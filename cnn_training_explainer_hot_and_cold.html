Demystifying Medical AI: Convolution, Loss, and Gradient Descent

The Core Intuition

AI doesn't "know" radiology. It plays Hot and Cold with millions of math dials.

When training a Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) to detect pneumonia, the computer starts with 100% blind random guesses. Every time it gets a scan wrong, the radiologist (the ground truth referee) yells "FREEZING COLD!". The algorithm slightly twists its internal dials toward "WARMER" (Gradient Descent). Repeat this millions of times, and the AI becomes an expert detector.

CHILD'S GAME AI EQUIVALENT
Blind guess Random Weights [a,b,c]
"Freezing cold!" High Loss / Error
"Take a step left" Gradient Descent Vector
"BURNING HOT!" Target Match (Loss ≈ 0)

Stage 02

AI Math: The Convolution Equation

The AI pairs each pixel with an adjustable variable or "dial" called a weight (a, b, c, d...).

Score = 110a + 118b + 255c + 29d + 119e + 235f + ... = 5,134.12 > Threshold (z)?

The Binary Decision Threshold

If the calculated dot-product score exceeds threshold z (e.g. 5,000), the network flags a pathology. Otherwise, it calls it normal.

Score > z
Pneumonia
Score ≤ z
Normal
The Big Mystery

Where do the magic numbers for a, b, c, d come from?

At first, nobody knows what values they should have! Not even the computer scientists. They start as completely random guesses (e.g., a = 0.042, b = -0.891). That's where the Hot and Cold game begins.
